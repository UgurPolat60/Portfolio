An end-to-end platform running the entire workflow of a certification and audit body: company records, certificate lifecycles, audit planning, expense tracking and reporting, all in one system. Laravel and Symfony were both available. I chose raw PDO and procedural PHP instead, and I can defend that decision.

Deliberately framework-free

Instead of Laravel or Symfony I chose raw PDO, procedural PHP and hand-written vanilla JS components. Each page is a small self-contained "mini-SPA" hosting its own AJAX endpoints. The goal was a system that deploys quickly onto shared hosting with minimum dependencies, and that a single engineer can hold entirely in their head. On a project like this, that is worth more than a service container.

Five roles, one session model

Admin, accounting manager, auditor and consultant — five distinct roles managed from a single session and security core, with the control panel redirecting itself according to the role that logged in.

The certificate lifecycle

A complete lifecycle starting from the company registry and moving through accreditation type, certifying body, document number, scope and validity dates. The tracking tower flags certificates that are expiring or already expired the moment you open it — which is the entire reason the business wanted software in the first place.

The data layer

20 tables on MySQL, mostly in 3NF with selective foreign keys. Evidence files are stored as binary (LONGBLOB); frequently-read counters use deliberately denormalised fields for performance. Every query goes through real prepared statements — not string concatenation with a hopeful addslashes().

Audit planning, expenses and reporting

Audit scheduling through the auditor field, tracking of consultancy and training expenses and collections, a compliance reporting panel, and a correspondence engine running on Monolog and PHPMailer — all built on the same session and authorisation core.
